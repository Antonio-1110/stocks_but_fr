How it works
  • Every trading day each stock gets two ratings from 1 to 5: short term (scored over {{range $i, $h := .Cfg.ShortHorizons}}{{if $i}} and {{end}}{{$h}}{{end}} trading days) and long term (over {{range $i, $h := .Cfg.LongHorizons}}{{if $i}} and {{end}}{{$h}}{{end}}).
  • Short term: mostly how close the stock is to its 52-week high (the one signal that held up in both alpha-lab periods), plus a bigger 1-month gain and a smaller 1-week move. Long term: 12-month gain (skipping the latest month), distance above the 200-day average, closeness to the 52-week high and, in Taiwan, the latest monthly revenue YoY known by its filing deadline.
  • Ratings rank stocks against each other on the day: 5 is the strongest fifth, 1 the weakest. So "Up" means "likely to beat most of the list", which in a falling market can still be a loss. The tables below check both: did up and down calls get the direction right, and did they beat the benchmark.
  • Each rating is saved with its date and never changed. Once its horizon has passed it is scored from the next trading day's close (time to act on it) to the close that many trading days later, dividends included.
  • Live rows are ratings made on the day. Backtest rows were rated after the fact on past days (every {{.Cfg.BackfillStep}}th trading day, about {{.Cfg.BackfillDays}} trading days back) with the same rules, to show accuracy from the start. US backtests use today's large caps, which flatters them (they're the ones that grew big).
